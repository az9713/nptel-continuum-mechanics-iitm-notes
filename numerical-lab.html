A numerical laboratory for deformation and objectivity

Rotate a sheared material element and compare exact Green strain with a linearized displacement-gradient calculation. The exact strain is unchanged by a superposed rigid rotation. The linearized result can change substantially because its small-gradient approximation excludes large rotations.

This is a prescribed two-dimensional kinematic construction embedded in three dimensions with the third stretch equal to one. It is not a force-driven simulation or an experimental measurement. The reference square has side one in dimensionless coordinates. All calculations run locally in the page; no input is sent to a server.

The model being calculated

In the plane, \(\mathbf S=\begin{pmatrix}1&\gamma\\0&1\end{pmatrix}\), \(\mathbf R=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\theta&-\sin\theta\\\sin\theta&\cos\theta\end{pmatrix}\), and F=RS. The Jacobian is 1. The exact Green strain is E=(FᵀF−I)/2=(SᵀS−I)/2. Its nonzero entries are E12=E21=γ/2 and E22=γ²/2. They depend on shear but not on θ.

The linearized tensor is ε=[(F−I)+(F−I)ᵀ]/2. For γ=0 and θ=90°, exact E is zero, while both planar diagonal entries of ε equal −1. This difference is an approximation failure, not physical compression caused by rotating the element. The approximation requires small displacement gradients, including small rigid rotation.

Three reproducible experiments

  1. Set γ=0 and rotate through 90°. The shape remains a square and Green strain stays zero, while the linearized tensor develops a large error.
  2. Hold γ=1 and vary θ. E12=0.5 and E22=0.5 stay fixed even as the spatial element rotates.
  3. Set θ=0 and reduce γ from 1 to 0.1. The omitted E22 term falls from 0.5 to 0.005, displaying its quadratic scaling.

Downloadable checks and limits

Download the Python laboratory. Running python numerical_lab.py checks rotation invariance, the finite versus linearized strain boundary, reference/current traction conversion and film power closure. These deterministic mathematical checks complement the interactive view; they do not establish material calibration, solve a general boundary-value problem or certify visual accessibility.
